FAQ

What it protects, and what it can't.

Do we both need to be online?

Yes. No server holds your messages, so a chat happens live, while you both have it open.

Can the other person see my IP address?

Yes. That's how direct connections work, and it's true through the relay too. On a shared network they can also see your device's local address, like 192.168.x.x.

Why does my phone ask about a relay?

Mobile data usually blocks direct connections, so a phone can fall back to our relay. The relay only ever sees encrypted data, both IP addresses and how much data flows. It keeps no logs. Computers always connect directly.

What happens when I open a link from a chat?

It opens in your browser, outside the chat, and that site sees your IP address. ecdh.chat asks before opening one and shows where it really goes.

Do disappearing messages really disappear?

From your screen, yes. Disappearing messages and delete for everyone clear the other screen too, but only if their app plays along. Anyone can take a photo of a screen.

Do files keep their details?

Photos are cleaned of location and camera details before sending. Other files, and photos sent as originals, arrive as they are, including the file name and things like the author of a document.

Does my browser remember the invite link?

It might. The page clears the link from the address bar, but your browser's history may still hold it. A used link is worthless, but a private window avoids the question entirely.

Does screenshot blocking stop everything?

No. On Windows it stops screenshots and screen sharing of the window, not programs that read the screen's text through accessibility features. Linux has no such protection.

Why does Windows warn about the installer?

The Windows installer isn't Authenticode-signed yet. Updates are checked against our own signing key, but the first install relies on downloading it from ecdh.chat over HTTPS. Only download it from here.

Do I have to trust your server?

On the website, yes: like any web app, the page comes from our server each time you open it. The desktop app ships its own copy of the code, and its source is public.

Can it protect me from malware?

No. Encryption can't protect against malware on a device, someone looking over your shoulder, or a camera pointed at the screen.
